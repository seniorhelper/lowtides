Agentic commerce needs consent from both sides

By Lowtides · November 11, 2026

Agentic commerce means software agents that shop and pay for a person. Most of the talk is about what the agent can do. The harder question is who agreed to it. Our answer: the shopper and the store both say yes, or no money moves.

What agentic commerce is

In ordinary online shopping a person clicks Buy. In agentic commerce an AI agent does the searching, the comparing and sometimes the checkout. The person sets a goal such as "a cordless drill under $90" and the agent carries it out.

That changes what consent means. A click was the consent. When an agent acts, the store sees an order but cannot see the person, and the person may not see the order until it is done.

Two consents are missing today

The shopper's consent, in a form a store can check

"My agent may spend up to $100 a day, never more than $60 at once, ask me over $25, never gift cards." Those are clear rules. But if they live only inside a chat window, the store has no way to verify them, and the agent can drift from them.

The store's consent

Stores have a say too. A store may welcome AI orders for some items and not others, cap order size, refuse resellers, or want a person to review every AI order. Today many stores can only block bots or accept everything.

What a consent handshake looks like

A handshake is a short, automatic check that happens before payment. Each side brings a signed statement:

  • The shopper's pass: who the agent acts for, its budget, its ask-me line, its never list and when the permission ends. It is cryptographically signed, so the store can check it has not been edited.
  • The store's policy: which agents it accepts, order limits, categories open to agents and whether a person reviews orders. Also signed.

Plain code compares the two and gives one of three answers:

  • Green, a match. Both sides' rules are met. The payment can go through.
  • Yellow, needs a yes. Something needs a person: the amount is over the ask-me line, or the store reviews AI orders. Nothing is charged while it waits.
  • Red, no match. A rule on either side says no. No money moves, and the receipt says which rule.

Why this is better for both sides

For shoppers: the agent cannot exceed limits you wrote, and you can read why every order was paid, held or blocked.

For stores: fewer disputes. An order that arrives with a verified pass shows the buyer set these limits and the order fit inside them. A store can turn agent orders off, review each one, or accept matches automatically.

For everyone: a stop switch that actually stops things, because every payment has to pass the same gate.

What consent is not

  • It is not a terms-of-service line saying anything the agent does is the customer's responsibility.
  • It is not a one-time "allow" that lasts forever. Good consent has limits and an end date, and can be pulled back at once.
  • It is not the AI deciding. The model can suggest. Code checks the rules.

Common questions

What is agentic commerce?

Agentic commerce is shopping carried out by AI agents on a person's behalf: the agent searches, compares and can complete the purchase within limits the person sets.

What is a consent handshake?

A consent handshake is an automatic check before payment that compares the shopper's signed limits with the store's signed policy. If both are met the order can be paid, if a person is needed it waits, and if either side says no, no money moves.

Can a store refuse AI shopping agents?

Yes. In a consent model the store sets its own policy: closed to AI shoppers, review every AI order, or accept orders that match automatically.
